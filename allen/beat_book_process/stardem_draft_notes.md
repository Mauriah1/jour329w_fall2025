For my second version I tried to achieve a beat book that more for someone who was new to maryland. However I want it written as an engaging, conversational magazine feature (no code or technical markup) that followed a specific narrative structure: welcoming intro, timeline of major 2024-2025 stories, thematic sections with examples, key people profiles, geographic equity analysis, reporter's toolkit, and practical tips—all grounded in real story data and quotes provided.  

The second beat book worked well in many ways. It captured a good narrative tone, immersing readers in the Eastern Shore with images of the Chesapeake Bay, Marydel during ICE raids, and the region’s historical landmarks. Events were also presented chronologically, making it easy to follow how the beat evolved from 2024 to 2025, including Federalsburg’s voting rights settlement, Pride Festival debates, and immigration enforcement actions. Major themes history, immigration, LGBTQ+ rights, and hate crimes, were also  clearly highlighted with examples, key players, and some discussion of coverage gaps. Profiles of Carl Snowden, Victoria Gomez Lozano, Jaelon Moaney, and Chief Donna Wolf Mother Abbott effectively show who shapes the beat and why, while geographic and demographic context helps situate the region’s diversity.

However, the guide was still overly long, wordy, and sometimes repetitive, especially in extended quotes and detailed event descriptions. The timeline was entirely textual, with no visual format to help reporters scan key events. Which is something I wanted. There were also coverage gaps and patterns existed as well such as frequently covered versus under covered stories, source diversity, or overlooked communities, are only implied rather than explicitly analyzed. The reporter toolkit and practical guidance were also minimal, lacking concise sections on essential terms, organizations, tips, and story opportunities. Geographic equity is hinted at but not clearly mapped or summarized, leaving some actionable insights buried in the text.

For the third, fourth and fifth prompt. I pushed to get models to produce me versions that were narrative but also something that would catch the readers eye while still covering the material that I wanted. If I were to do this all over again I would start with the gemini or anthropic models.
