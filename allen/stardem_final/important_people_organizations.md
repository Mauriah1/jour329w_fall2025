Important People & Organizations Reference Document
Sources Mentioned 3+ Times in Race & Diversity Coverage (2023-2025)
This document identifies key sources for beat coverage based on frequency of mention across stories.

PEOPLE (Mentioned 3+ Times)
Carl Snowden
Convener, Caucus of African American Leaders
Significant In Anne Arundel County 

Why Important: Central figure in nearly every major civil rights action on the Shore—from bridge renaming efforts to the Federalsburg voting rights case to organizing responses to racial incidents; acts as spokesperson and organizer for regional civil rights coalition.

Victoria Gomez Lozano
Deputy Director, Chesapeake Multicultural Resource Center; Governor's Commission on Hispanic Affairs
Significant In Talbot County 

Why Important: Primary contact for Hispanic/Latino community issues on the Shore; coordinates immigration services, cultural events, and responds to immigration enforcement—essential source for fastest-growing demographic group in the region.

Tina Jones
Co-founder and Board Chair, Delmarva Pride Center

Why Important: Leading voice for LGBTQ+ community across the Shore; speaks at government meetings, organizes Pride events, and responds to discrimination issues—key source for LGBTQ+ stories and DEI controversies.

Kyle O'Donnell
Co-founder, Delmarva Pride Center Board member

Why Important: Co-organizer of annual Pride Festival (now in 4th year) and spokesperson for LGBTQ+ issues; provides historical context on Pride movement's growth and challenges in rural communities.

Matthew Peters
Executive Director, Chesapeake Multicultural Resource Center

Why Important: Leads organization serving ~3,500 people annually; tracks immigration policy impacts, coordinates legal services, and serves as primary source for understanding how federal immigration actions affect local Hispanic communities.

Jaelon Moaney
Vice Chair, Maryland Commission on African American History and Culture
- Significant in Dorchester County 

Why Important: Youngest member of state commission overseeing $5 million in African American heritage grants; native son with deep Eastern Shore roots who bridges state policy and local history—frequently speaks at major commemorative events.

Dave Stepp
- Talbot County Council member

Why Important: Proposed controversial repeal of county's DEI statement (2025); represents conservative viewpoint on diversity initiatives and opposes what he calls "politicized DEI"—key voice in ongoing DEI debates affecting local government.

Keasha Haythe
- Talbot County Council member

Why Important: Only African American woman on Talbot County Council; opposed DEI statement repeal and represents marginalized communities' perspective in county government—essential voice on inclusion issues.

Lajan Cephas
Mayor of Cambridge

Why Important: Second Black female mayor of Cambridge from prominent multi-generational Dorchester County family; oversees city's response to "Just Communities" designation and represents new generation of Black political leadership on Shore.

Willie Woods
President, Caroline County NAACP

Why Important: Led NAACP involvement in historic Federalsburg voting rights settlement; coordinates civil rights responses in Caroline County where Hispanic population has grown dramatically in small towns.

Daniel Fuentes Espinal
Pastor, Iglesia del Nazareno Jesús Te Ama (detained by ICE July 2025)

Why Important: His detention after 24 years in Easton galvanized community protests and became focal point for debate over Trump administration immigration enforcement—symbol of how federal policies affect established immigrant families.

Frank Gunsallus
Easton Town Council President

Why Important: Called Pride flag "antithetical to America" and opposed Pride Festival funding; represents vocal opposition to LGBTQ+ initiatives—flashpoint in culture war debates affecting town policy.


ORGANIZATIONS (Mentioned 3+ Times)
Chesapeake Multicultural Resource Center (ChesMRC)
Location: EASTON
Contact: 877-772-9832 | chesmrc.org

Why Important: Front-line organization serving Hispanic/Latino immigrants with legal services, citizenship classes, and emergency support; tracks ICE activity and responds to immigration crises—essential for understanding how federal immigration policy affects Shore communities.

Delmarva Pride Center
Location: Easton (with chapters forming in Dorchester and Kent counties)

Why Important: Only regional LGBTQ+ organization serving rural Shore; organizes Pride festivals, support groups, and advocacy efforts; expanding chapter model indicates growing LGBTQ+ visibility in conservative areas—barometer for LGBTQ+ acceptance.

Caucus of African American Leaders
Statewide consortium including NAACP, United Black Clergy
Eastern Shore Convener: Rev. James Jones

Why Important: Coordinating body for major civil rights actions including Federalsburg voting rights case, monument removal efforts, and bridge renamings; brings statewide resources to local issues—go-to organization for organized civil rights responses.

NAACP County Branches
Caroline County: Willie Woods, President
Talbot County: Richard Potter, President
Kent County, Dorchester County

Why Important: Local chapters involved in voting rights litigation, police accountability, and community justice issues; provide organizational structure and legal support for civil rights complaints—necessary contacts for discrimination cases.

Frederick Douglass Honor Society
Location: Easton
Organizes: Annual Frederick Douglass Day

Why Important: Maintains visibility of Douglass's legacy through annual celebrations drawing hundreds; advocates for African American history education and memorialization—represents ongoing effort to center Black history in predominantly white spaces.

Kennard African American Cultural Heritage Center
Location: Centreville
President: Clayton Washington

Why Important: Queen Anne's County's repository of African American history; hosts Juneteenth, lectures, and recently unveiled plaque honoring U.S. Colored Troops—one of few institutions preserving and presenting Black history in rural county.

Talbot County Democratic Forum
President: Michele Dappert

Why Important: Organized protests against ICE arrests and pastor's detention; mobilizes progressive community responses to Trump administration policies—represents organized liberal opposition in otherwise conservative county.
