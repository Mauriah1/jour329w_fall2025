Star-Dem Entities 11/05/25

For this assignment, I used two different models to extract entities from Star-Democrat stories. My first model was groq/moonshotai/kimi-k2-instruct-0905, and for this one, I basically told it to construct and identify entities from the articles, letting it determine what to include. The second model I used was groq/openai/gpt-oss-120b, and for this prompt, I asked the model to produce code that would extract all of the important entities, using a similar approach to the first model.

The results I got from both models were moderate and surprisingly similar to each other. Both models did well at identifying metadata fields like title, date, author, docref, article ID, year, month, and day. These technical elements were consistently accurate across both approaches when faceting and when I looked at the data on dataset. However, both models struggled significantly with the core entity types that would be interesting to see: people, organizations, and places. They had trouble consistently identifying names of individuals, missed organizational entities, and couldn't reliably extract geographic locations.

When I manually checked several stories by reading their full text and comparing what was actually there versus what the models extracted, I found that the models were often missing key entities. For example, in stories where mayors, council members, and government officials were clearly mentioned and even quoted, the models sometimes failed to identify these people as entities. Similarly, specific organizations like chambers of commerce or government agencies weren't always captured, and place names that were central to the stories were frequently overlooked.

What was interesting was how different the two datasets looked from last week to this week. Although they both showed the same strengths in metadata extraction there was a signifcant change  in entity identification. This pattern suggested to me that the issue wasn't really about which model I chose, but rather about how I designed my prompts. Neither model had clear, concrete examples to follow, so they were essentially guessing at what I wanted.However what became apparent to me was that, I had much better results last week because I provided more specific guidance and had more control over the prompt design. I gave the model explicit examples of what I wanted, and the outputs were more accurate and consistent. This week, I gave more control to the LLM by being less prescriptive, and the quality suffered as a result.

One particular issue that stood out was the content source column. When I merged the two datasets from the previous assignment with my entities file, everything was labeled as "full document," which wasn't helpful or what I intended. This was clearly a case where I needed to provide more specific instructions about how to classify the source of the content

What I learned from this experience is that providing specific examples within the prompt is crucial for getting good results. When I let the LLM interpret the task on its own without concrete examples, it defaulted to what it could easily identify, structured metadata, rather than the more nuanced work of entity extraction that requires understanding context and importance. If I were to do this again, I would include explicit examples in my prompt showing exactly what kinds of entities I want extracted, what format they should be in, and what constitutes an "important" entity versus a passing mention.

Overall, both models did well with tagging things they could easily understand from structure and format, but they struggled with the contextual understanding needed to identify people, places, and organizations from narrative text. The best approach for this task would be to provide your own clear examples with the prompt, defining exactly what you want and showing the model several instances of correct extraction. Without that guidance, even different models with different capabilities end up producing similar results.
