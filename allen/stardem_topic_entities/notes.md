For this assignment, I worked with two different models to extract entities from Star-Democrat race and diversity stories: groq/meta-llama/llama-4-maverick-17b-128e-instruct and groq/openai/gpt-oss-120b.

The prompt I created for my first run was significantly more specified than anything I had used in the prior class. This time, I really wanted it to show me geographic areas in detail while also understanding the types of people and organizations involved in these race and diversity stories.I wanted context, titles, roles, and relationships. I included specific examples in this version, showing the model exactly what I meant by "extract people with titles" by giving examples like "Mayor John Smith" and "Rev. James Williams." I also included the Maryland county reference list that Cat provided, which mapped municipalities to their counties. This was crucial because I wanted to know not just that a story mentioned "Cambridge," but that Cambridge is in Dorchester County.

The results were remarkably better than my previous assignment. When stories mentioned "Cambridge," it correctly identified Dorchester County. When "Easton" appeared, it knew that was Talbot County. However, there were still problems: the content_type field kept returning "full document" instead of useful categories, and I was getting too many "N/A" values for people, organizations, and geographic information in stories where these entities clearly existed.

For the second iteration, I made changes. I expanded content_type from just two options to fourteen specific categories: news article, feature article, enterprise story, profile, analysis, sports story, arts/culture, opinion, obituary, calendar listing, legal notice, announcement, brief, and other. I also added strong emphasis with "CRITICAL - DO NOT USE N/A UNLESS ABSOLUTELY CERTAIN" and provided detailed instructions with multiple search strategies for finding people, organizations, and authors.

The second prompt produced the best results I'd had so far, though it took the longest to process. Content types were now specific and useful, I could see what percentage of coverage was news articles, what percetange was features, and etc. The N/A problem improved, but not drastically people and organizations were still heavily n/a. 

But here's what puzzled me: the second version still had trouble finding authors, which I thought was odd because the versions I did prior to this assignment were worse in terms of content types and overall quality, but they still found the authors more reliably. It seemed like maybe I had overwhelmed the model with too many instructions, causing it to focus less on the byline while searching for all the people mentioned in the story itself.

When I examined 5-10 stories in detail, I found that basic elements like dates and titles were consistently accurate, and there were no false positives, the model wasn't inventing entities. However, significant problems emerged immediately. The most significant issue was that authors were completely missing from the author field even though their bylines appeared clearly in the content files. For example, Tony Woods wrote "Trump rebrands 'Don't Ask, Don't Tell' policy," but the author field showed "N/A." This wasn't an isolated case, it was like this for all of the stories.

The model also struggled with completeness. It missed numerous people mentioned in the stories, including Ronald Reagan in the MLK story, Julie Schablitsky (Chief Archaeologist for Maryland Department of Transportation) in the Harriet Tubman story, and multiple people in the Caroline County MLK story including student Wanda Molock and Commissioner Frank Bartz. Geographic details were sometimes missed too, like Rochester, New York in the Frederick Douglass story. Content type classification was oversimplified, narrative stories were just labeled "full article" rather than recognizing their storytelling approach.

When I compared the two models directly, I noticed some interesting differences the GPT-OSS-120b model was significantly faster quicker than the llama model. It followed structured instructions well and was excellent at using the county reference list for geographic matching. However, it was more likely to return N/A when it wasn't completely certain about something, and sometimes it was inconsistent with formatting. One story might have people listed as "Mayor Smith; Dr. Jones" and another might format it slightly differently.

The Llama model (groq/meta-llama/llama-4-maverick-17b-128e-instruct) , on the other hand, was slower but more thorough. It was better at finding people and organizations hidden in complex sentence structures. The formatting was more consistent across all stories, and it was superior at distinguishing between the different content types, it seemed to understand the difference between a news article and a feature article more intuitively. It was also less likely to give up and return N/A when information was there but not obvious. For the final analysis, I felt the improved accuracy from this model was worth the extra processing time, even though neither model solved the author extraction problem as well as I hoped.  

What the model did extract, it extracted correctly. People's names were accurate when found, and geographic data like Dorchester County and Talbot County was consistent. Talbot County was also the most frequent mentioned place with 32 mentions. The Organizations like NAACP and Delmarva Pride Center were properly identified when captured. These two were also the top two organozations mentioned in the articles (they had 3 mentions each). The patterns made sense for my topic, it mostly being in Talbot County made sense because the papaer is made there and also the organizations made sense as well becasue they were organization that fought for equality for people. 

The core problem became clear: the LLM wasn't systematically reading through the content files and transferring information to the structured fields. All the data existed in the content, authors, people, places, organizations, but the model processed each field independently rather than comprehensively scanning the narrative text. For building a functional beat book, I would need to create a model that systematically extracts all information from the content file and properly distributes it across metadata fields, with accurate filters in Datasette to make the data actually searchable and useful for journalistic research.
